A UPI study from NPCI's statistics · 2016 to 2026

Bengaluru does more UPI than 31 states.

712 million payments in one month, from one district. UPI is 25 billion payments a month, and it is lopsided: a few cities, two apps and a flood of tiny payments. Seven charts on where it really happens.

01

In most states, one city does most of the paying.

The ten biggest states by payments, September 2026. Coloured segments are the districts that make NPCI's national top 30. Gray is the rest of the state.

So what

Bengaluru Urban alone did 712 million payments in September, more than 31 of India's 36 states and union territories.

Maharashtra is three quarters Mumbai, Pune, Thane and friends. Karnataka is 57% Bengaluru. Telangana is two thirds the Hyderabad cluster. Uttar Pradesh, the most populous state, is the exception: its three top-30 districts add up to a third, and the rest is spread across 72 others.

One caveat that should sit on every geography chart of UPI: 44% of all payments carry no state at all in NPCI's data. The ranking is of the classified half.

02

UPI adds the same four billion payments every year.

How many extra monthly payments UPI picked up each year, October to October. The bar is the addition; the small number is the growth rate it implies.

So what

A straight line in volume is a falling line in percent.

From 2023 the bar stops getting taller. UPI adds four to five billion monthly payments every year no matter how big it already is, so growth rates slide from 56% to 23% without anything actually slowing down.

If you only read the growth rate you would call this a maturing product. If you only read the bar you would call it a steady machine. Both are true.

03

Only one month in ten years went backwards: April 2020.

Every month since 2018 as a cell. Colour is the change from the month before. Blue grew, orange shrank.

So what

The lockdown is the only real crash.

April 2020 lost a fifth of all payments in one month. Everything else in orange is calendar arithmetic: February has three fewer days, and November gives back part of the Diwali bump in October.

The grid also shows the compounding fade from chapter 2 another way. The early rows are deep blue almost everywhere. By 2025 a typical month is a pale 1 to 3%.

04

The average payment is shrinking. Shop payments are the reason.

Average rupees per payment, split by who is being paid: a person, or a merchant. The blended line is what NPCI's headline numbers imply.

So what

This is a mix effect.

A payment to a person has stayed around ₹2,400 for five years. A payment to a merchant drifted from ₹886 to ₹603. The blended average fell from ₹1,800 to ₹1,238 mostly because the merchant share of payments went from 40% to 63%, the strip under the chart.

Merchant payments grew 8.7× in that window. Person-to-person grew 3.4×. The new payments are the small cash purchases that moved onto the phone.

05

Shops get many tiny payments. Finance gets a few big ones.

Every merchant category NPCI reports for September 2026. Width is its share of merchant payments. Height is the average ticket. Area is the money.

So what

Groceries alone are a quarter of merchant payments, at ₹214 each.

Add fast food, restaurants, bakeries, dairies and cigarette stands and you have more than half of all merchant taps averaging under ₹250. That is the slab on the left: the everyday purchases that were coins and small notes until recently.

On the right, securities brokers take 0.6% of taps but 8.4% of the money, at ₹8,090 a go. Debt collection is 1.5% of taps and 8.6% of the money. The needle and the slab are the same product carrying two different economies.

06

Every app, bank and state, ranked by the size of one payment.

Merchant categories, apps, banks and states on the same log scale of average ticket. The dashed lines are the two halves of the network from chapter 4.

So what

Ticket size tells you who something is for.

FamApp, a teen app, runs at ₹132. Navi and super.money sit at ₹483, the signature of cashback-driven small spends. Cred clears credit-card bills at ₹3,881. India Post Payments Bank at ₹477 is the rural post office counter; HDFC at ₹1,880 is the urban salary account.

States barely spread at all. Assam at ₹996 and Ladakh at ₹1,898 bracket everyone, and most sit within ₹200 of each other. Geography is not where the variation in UPI lives. Purpose is.

07

PhonePe and Google Pay own 79%. The rest is growing anyway.

Share of payments by app, January 2022 to September 2026. The two incumbents are drawn in gray so the movement shows. Hover a name to isolate it.

So what

Paytm's cliff is a regulator, not a competitor.

Its share went from 13% to 8% between January and April 2024, when RBI shut down Paytm Payments Bank. Almost all of it went to PhonePe and Google Pay, whose combined share peaked at 87%.

Since then the pair has slipped back to 79%, and the gain went to apps that did not exist in this table in 2023. Navi is at 4.8% and super.money at 1.7%, both with tickets under ₹500. The long tail went from 1.4% to 4.7%, and the number of live apps from 59 to 96.

Method

Average ticket is value divided by volume. Merchant categories use NPCI's MCC grouping; "Others" is their bucket. 44% of state-wise volume is unclassified in the source and is left out of chapter 1. Charts are D3 drawn from a static copy of the data; they redraw on resize and follow your system theme.
